# Concept Map: Course Concepts in the RBGS Paper

**Paper:** O. Balabanov and L. Grigori, *Randomized Block Gram–Schmidt Process for the Solution of Linear Systems and Eigenvalue Problems*, arXiv:2111.14641v2 (27 May 2023).

Page numbers are the ones printed at the bottom of the arXiv v2 PDF. The supplementary material (Lemma SM.1 and the notes on least squares and Rayleigh–Ritz) is on pp. 29–33 of the same file and is cited as "Suppl."

| Course concept | Where it appears in the paper | How it is used |
|---|---|---|
| QR factorization | Sec. 1, $`W=QR`$ (p. 1); Algorithm 1.1, BGS (p. 3); Algorithm 2.1, RBGS (p. 7); Algorithm 4.1, RBGS-Arnoldi (p. 14) | The aim is $`W=QR`$ with $`Q`$ orthonormal (or very well conditioned), $`\mathrm{range}(Q)=\mathrm{range}(W)`$ and $`R`$ upper triangular. RBGS works one block of columns at a time: it projects the new block against the earlier ones and then QR-factors what is left (steps 3–5 of Algorithm 2.1). In the Arnoldi setting, the Hessenberg matrix $`H`$ is the $`R`$ factor of $`[B,\ AQ_{(1:p-1)}]`$ without its first block column (p. 14). |
| Orthogonal projection | Sec. 1.2, projectors of BCGS and BMGS, eq. (1.1) (pp. 3–4); step 3 of Algorithm 2.1 (p. 7); the projector $`\Pi^{\Theta}_{Q_{p-1}}`$ in Sec. 4.4 (p. 16); (SM.3)–(SM.5) (pp. 30–31) | Each new block is made orthogonal to the previous ones with a projector that approximates the $`\ell_2`$-orthogonal projector onto $`\mathrm{range}(Q_{(1:i-1)})^{\perp}`$. Rounding errors in this step cause the loss of orthogonality seen in BCGS and BMGS. RBGS takes the coefficients $`R_{(1:i-1,i)}`$ from a sketched problem, so its output is orthogonal in the sketched inner product $`\langle\Theta\cdot,\Theta\cdot\rangle`$. The same sketched projector returns in the Rayleigh–Ritz analysis. |
| Least squares | Eqs. (2.1) and (2.2) (p. 6); step 2 of Algorithm 2.1 and Assumptions 2.2 (pp. 7–8); eq. (4.2) for GMRES (p. 14); Algorithm SM.1 (p. 29) | Computing the projection coefficients is the tall least-squares problem $`\min_Y \lVert Q_{(1:i-1)}Y-W_{(i)}\rVert_F`$ in (2.1). RBGS solves the $`k`$-row sketched version $`\min_Y \lVert \Theta Q_{(1:i-1)}Y-\Theta W_{(i)}\rVert_F`$ in (2.2), $`k\ll n`$, and asks the solver to be backward stable (Assumptions 2.2). GMRES in (4.2) is also a small least-squares problem, in the Hessenberg coordinates. |
| Singular values and condition number | Notation in Sec. 1.1 (p. 2); Corollary 1.4 (p. 4); eq. (2.5) (p. 8); Theorem 3.2 (p. 12, proof p. 21); Theorem 3.4 (p. 12); Proposition 3.5 (p. 13); eq. (4.8) (p. 16) | $`\sigma_{\min}`$ and $`\sigma_{\max}`$ measure how much a matrix can shrink or stretch a vector, and $`\mathrm{cond}=\sigma_{\max}/\sigma_{\min}`$. Corollary 1.4 turns singular-value information about the small sketch $`\Theta Q`$ into information about $`Q`$. Theorem 3.2 uses it to show $`\hat Q`$ is well conditioned, and the factor $`\mathrm{cond}(\hat Q)`$ is what appears in the GMRES quasi-optimality bound (p. 14). The a priori results also depend on $`\mathrm{cond}(\hat W)`$. |
| Cholesky factorization / Cholesky QR | Remark 2.1 (p. 7); RCholeskyQR, Sec. 2.2.2 with Algorithms 2.2 and 2.3 (pp. 8–9); implicit version, Algorithm 2.4 (p. 10); Algorithm 2.5 (p. 10) | Classical Cholesky QR finds $`R'`$ with $`R'^{\top}R'=Q^{\top}Q`$ and sets $`Q\leftarrow QR'^{-1}`$. Remark 2.1 applies it to the output of RBGS to get a factor that is $`\ell_2`$-orthonormal up to machine precision. RCholeskyQR is the sketched analogue used for the inter-block QR in steps 4–5: $`R_{(i,i)}`$ comes from an ordinary QR of the small matrix $`\Theta Q'_{(i)}`$, and then $`Q_{(i)}=Q'_{(i)}R_{(i,i)}^{-1}`$ by forward substitution. The implicit variant needs only one global synchronization per block. |
| Random embedding / sketching | Sec. 1.3: Definitions 1.1–1.2, Corollaries 1.3–1.4, Rademacher and SRHT matrices (pp. 4–5); Corollary 1.5 (p. 5); Proposition 3.5 (p. 13) | $`\Theta\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times n}`$ is an $`\varepsilon`$-embedding for a subspace if it preserves inner products there up to $`\varepsilon\lVert x\rVert\lVert y\rVert`$ (Def. 1.1). An oblivious embedding (Def. 1.2) achieves this with high probability for any fixed subspace of the right dimension. This is why the sketched problems are faithful stand-ins for the original ones. Corollary 1.3 bounds $`\lVert\Theta\rVert_F`$, which is used in (3.2). |
| Floating-point error and mixed precision | Sec. 1.4, eqs. (1.3)–(1.6) (pp. 5–6); the precision labels $`u_{\mathrm{crs}}`$ and $`u_{\mathrm{fine}}`$ in Algorithm 2.1 (p. 7); Assumptions 3.1 (p. 11); Theorems 3.2 and 3.4 (p. 12); float32/float64 experiments in Sec. 5 (p. 18) | Corollary 1.5 shows that, under the probabilistic rounding model, the sketched rounding error is bounded independently of $`n`$. So the expensive high-dimensional step 3 can run with a coarse unit roundoff $`u_{\mathrm{crs}}`$, while the cheap small steps use $`u_{\mathrm{fine}}\le u_{\mathrm{crs}}`$. The stability results then require only conditions like $`u_{\mathrm{crs}}=O(m^{-3/2})`$ for certification (pp. 7, 12), with no dependence on $`n`$. |
| Eigenvalues and Rayleigh–Ritz | Sec. 4.4, eqs. (4.6)–(4.9) (pp. 15–16); Algorithm 4.2 (p. 17); Suppl.: Lemma SM.1, Theorems SM.2–SM.3, Corollary SM.4 (pp. 30–33); numerical test in Sec. 5.3 (pp. 20–21) | Rayleigh–Ritz takes eigenpairs $`(\mu,y)`$ of the small matrix $`H_{(1:p-1,1:p-1)}`$ and returns $`(\mu, Q_{(1:p-1)}y)`$ as approximate eigenpairs of $`A`$. With a $`\Theta`$-orthonormal basis the Galerkin condition becomes the sketched condition (4.9). Its accuracy is analysed in the supplement through projection operators. |
| Dimension reduction / low-rank structure | Krylov subspace $`\mathcal{K}^{(j)}(A,B)`$ (p. 13); sketch size $`k\ll n`$ in Algorithm 2.1 (p. 7); numerically rank-deficient test matrix in Sec. 5.1 (p. 18); future work on model order reduction and compression in Sec. 7 (p. 26) | The paper does not prove a low-rank approximation theorem, so this concept should be read loosely. Two reductions are used. Sketching replaces $`n`$-dimensional projections and least-squares problems by $`k`$-dimensional ones. The Krylov subspace replaces $`AX=B`$ or $`AX=X\Lambda`$ by a small projected problem. Sec. 5.1 tests RBGS on a matrix whose leading columns are numerically rank deficient. |
| GMRES and residual minimization | Sec. 4.2, eq. (4.2) and the quasi-optimality argument (p. 14); Remark 4.1 (p. 15); Sec. 5.2 (pp. 19–20) | GMRES picks the vector in the Krylov subspace with the smallest residual. With a $`\Theta`$-orthonormal basis this equals minimizing the sketched residual. Under the stability conditions of RBGS, the computed solution minimizes the residual up to a factor $`\mathrm{cond}(\hat Q)=O(1)`$, for a slightly perturbed matrix $`A+\Delta A`$. |

## How the pieces connect

RBGS produces a QR factorization $`W\approx QR`$ in which all the large-dimensional work is replaced by work on the sketches $`\Theta W`$ and $`\Theta Q`$. The embedding property (Sec. 1.3, Corollary 1.4) is what lets the small sketched computations stand in for the original ones, and the stability analysis (Sec. 3) shows that this still holds in floating point, with the dominant operations in coarse precision. Applying RBGS to the columns $`B, AQ, \dots`$ gives the randomized Arnoldi basis of Sec. 4, which is then used for GMRES (linear systems) and Rayleigh–Ritz (eigenvalue problems).
